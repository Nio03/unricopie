On the blog we note when a project lists Claude as a co-author of its commits, and the catalogue has a "human-driven" tag for anyone who wants to filter by it. It's only fair to hold ourselves to the same standard: Recompendium and Unricopie are made with the help of AI. This is what it does and what it doesn't.

## What we use it for

- **The code.** The code of the site and of Unricopie is written with Claude, by Anthropic, under our direction: we decide what gets done and how, and we review and test it before publishing. In the site's repository, the commits it took part in list it as a co-author.
- **Finding new projects.** It helps us track repositories and news from the scene, which are then reviewed one by one.
- **Drafting.** The entries are drafted with its help, and so is their translation between Spanish and English.

## What it doesn't do

- **It doesn't decide what goes into the catalogue.** Each entry is checked by hand against its project's README: the section, the status, and what it requires are read there, not from the project's name or from how it was announced.
- **It doesn't make up data.** Stars, versions, and dates come from GitHub, and a bot updates them every day.
- **It doesn't generate the covers.** The game covers are the real ones.

## Why we say so

Because it's what we ask of others. Knowing how a project was made helps you decide whether to trust it, and that information is most useful when nobody hides it. If we measure others with that yardstick, we have to go through it first.

## What it means for you

- **Unricopie is open source,** under the GPL-3.0: anyone can read on [GitHub](https://github.com/Nio03/unricopie-launcher) what it does before installing it.
- **If you find a mistake,** in an entry, on the site, or in the launcher, tell us on [GitHub](https://github.com/Nio03/unricopie/issues). We'll look into it and fix it.
