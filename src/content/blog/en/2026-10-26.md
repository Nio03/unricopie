## Super Mario Maker 2, recompiled in a week

On October 19, SMM2 Recomp put out its first release, a static recompilation of Super Mario Maker 2 ([entry](/en/recomp/super-mario-maker-2/), [GitHub](https://github.com/smm2recomp/smm2recomp)). What you download is a launcher with the recompiler, not the game: on first start it checks your dump of version 3.0.3, translates the game’s AArch64 code to C, and compiles it on your machine. In one week it shipped 19 releases, from 0.1.0 to 0.5.5 on October 25, with packages for Windows, Linux, and Apple Silicon Macs.

On top of the game, it adds the 100 Mario Challenge from Super Mario Maker 1, a Course World that doesn’t depend on Nintendo’s servers, co-op in Endless Challenge, and editing for any downloaded course. 92 of its last 100 commits list Claude as a co-author.

It wasn’t the only new Switch project. On October 21, EoWRecomp released alpha 1 of its recompilation of The Legend of Zelda: Echoes of Wisdom ([entry](/en/recomp/the-legend-of-zelda-echoes-of-wisdom/), [GitHub](https://github.com/Nycolazs/EoWRecomp)), which builds on LAS Recomp, the Link’s Awakening project we covered last week. It isn’t playable yet: the alpha is a preview of the app for macOS and Android, which sets the game up from your own files, and the recompiled game has only reached, on Linux, the prologue and the first controllable scene. Its author states that it’s developed almost entirely with AI, through Claude Code.

Three Switch recomps released the week before also joined the catalog this week: [The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword HD](/en/recomp/the-legend-of-zelda-skyward-sword-hd/) and [Bayonetta 2](/en/recomp/bayonetta-2/), both from October 17, and [Pokémon: Let’s Go, Pikachu! / Let’s Go, Eevee!](/en/recomp/pokemon-lets-go-pikachu-eevee/), from the 16th.

## Gravity Rush and more PS4 ports

The family of PS4 ports that started with Bloodborne and God of War III keeps growing: the game’s x86-64 code runs directly on the PC’s CPU, the console’s system is reimplemented, and the graphics go to Vulkan. All of them need your own dump of the game.

- **[Gravity Rush Remastered](/en/port/gravity-rush-remastered/)**: LucaPazienza’s port put out its first preview on October 17, and with 0.1.8, on the 23rd, the whole story has been played from start to finish, with sound and saves. It renders at up to 4K, supports FSR 3.1 and DLSS, and the DualSense gyro steers the gravity camera as on the PS4. Video Game Esoterica covered it in a video on the 24th ([video](https://youtu.be/NLl-E2gaUfU)). So far it has only been tested on Nvidia graphics cards.
- **[Unicorn Overlord](/en/port/unicorn-overlord/)** (October 19): with the Asian release and the 1.04 update in English, the prologue, chapter 1, and its first battle have been played, and saving and loading work. The game’s own options gain a PC section with resolution, FSR 1, and PlayStation, Xbox, or Nintendo button icons.
- **[inFAMOUS Second Son](/en/port/infamous-second-son-castlesea/)** (October 19): Castlesea’s Windows port already plays story missions and lets you roam the open world, at about 80 to 95 fps in the city on a high-end PC. It has a launcher with FSR 1, a frame limit, and a separate window for using the touchpad and the graffiti spray can with the mouse. It’s the second port of this game: [HayatoG’s](/en/port/infamous-second-son-hayatog/), for Linux and Windows, has been releasing builds since October 14.

From the same author, HayatoG, and the same October 14, the ports of [The Last Guardian](/en/port/the-last-guardian/) and [Gravity Rush 2](/en/port/gravity-rush-2/) joined the catalog. Other PS4 ports from the week before arrived too: [Driveclub](/en/port/driveclub-overkilllabs3/), with racing wheels and output up to 8K; [Dragon’s Crown Pro](/en/port/dragons-crown-pro/); [EA Sports UFC 3](/en/port/ea-sports-ufc-3/); [MediEvil](/en/port/medievil-ps4/), the 2019 remake; [Crash Team Racing Nitro-Fueled](/en/port/crash-team-racing-nitro-fueled/); [another take](/en/port/uncharted-the-nathan-drake-collection-bc160201983/) on Uncharted: The Nathan Drake Collection; and three alternatives for Bloodborne: [Lantern Engine](/en/port/bloodborne-lantern-engine/), [bbhost](/en/port/bloodborne-droogie/), and a [macOS version](/en/port/bloodborne-macos/).

## From the Xbox and the Xbox 360

- **[Crash of the Titans](/en/recomp/crash-of-the-titans-crashrecomp/)** (October 22): CrashRecomp, a Windows “Definitive Edition” recompiled from the Xbox 360 version with XenonRecomp. It goes up to 4K internally, with DLSS, DLAA, or FSR 3.1, and adds subtitles for every line of dialogue and every video, plus a Brazilian Portuguese translation. It shipped seven releases in four days: with 1.04, on the 24th, the heaviest scene went from about 180 to about 500 fps on its test machine.
- **[Project Gotham Racing](/en/recomp/project-gotham-racing/)** (October 23): version 1.0 of this static recompilation of the original Xbox game, made with Xbox XBE Recomp. The only thing published is a launcher that builds the game on your PC from your European disc, with about 1.4 GB of build tools and 30 to 90 minutes the first time. It runs at 60 fps, with widescreen and ultrawide and a render scale from 25 to 200%. Version 1.1 came out on the 26th.
- **[FIFA 09](/en/recomp/fifa-09/)** (October 20): a Windows port of the Xbox 360 version, made with ReXGlue, with the online modes back on free community servers: ranked matches, leagues, and in-game friends. Version 1.4.0 is from the 23rd.
- **[Mortal Kombat: Shaolin Monks](/en/recomp/mortal-kombat-shaolin-monks/)**: recompiled from the Xbox with XboxXBE-Recomp, for Windows and, since October 19, Linux too. Its author says they’ve finished the whole campaign, though graphical glitches remain. Version 0.3.0 came out on the 25th.
- **[Midnight Club 3: DUB Edition](/en/recomp/midnight-club-3-dub-edition/)** (October 21): a recompilation of the Xbox version with xboxrecomp that, besides Linux, macOS, and Windows, reaches the homebrew Nintendo Switch, where the career can be completed at 20 to 25 fps. The published releases are the Switch ones; on computers you build it from source.
- **[Call of Duty: Finest Hour](/en/recomp/call-of-duty-finest-hour/)** (October 17): Finest Hour Remastered, recompiled from the Xbox with a new renderer that adds per-pixel lighting, dynamic shadows, and reflections. The downloads include AI-remastered textures.

## More news

- **[Sin and Punishment](/en/recomp/sin-and-punishment/)** (October 22): a recompilation of the Nintendo 64 game with N64Recomp and RT64, with widescreen, mouse aiming, and dual-stick controllers. By the 25th it was already on 0.2.3.
- **[Virtua Fighter 3](/en/recomp/virtua-fighter-3/)** (October 19): mr-finster, who made the Virtua Fighter 1 and 2 recompilations, started releasing alphas of the Sega Model 3 arcade game, with render scaling up to 6x and up to 120 frames per second.
- **[Super Mario All-Stars](/en/recomp/super-mario-all-stars/)** (October 20): the Super Nintendo collection, recompiled with snesrecomp for macOS, Windows, and Linux. The next day, 0.2.0 added Android, with an on-screen SNES controller.
- **[Monster Hunter Freedom Unite](/en/recomp/monster-hunter-freedom-unite/)** (October 19): MHForever recompiles the PSP game from your disc image, at 30 fps or, experimentally, at 60 to 180 with frame interpolation. Only the European release for now.
- **[Torchlight](/en/port/torchlight/)** (October 20): Torchlight Rekindled, an engine that follows the logic of the Xbox 360 port and runs the game on Windows, Linux, Android, iOS, and the homebrew Switch, from the files of your PC or Xbox 360 copy.
- **[inFAMOUS](/en/recomp/infamous/)** (October 18, at the tail end of last week): a Windows port of the European PS3 release, made with ps3recomp. The installer regenerates the code from your copy and compiles it on your machine.

## Browser ports that don’t ship the game

Several PC ports that also run in the browser joined the catalog this week. None of them ships a full commercial game: they use data that can be shared freely, and for the whole game they ask for your own copy.

- **[Jazz Jackrabbit 2](/en/port/jazz-jackrabbit-2-resurrection/)**: Jazz² Resurrection’s web version ([de4th.dev/jazz2/wasm](https://de4th.dev/jazz2/wasm/)) uses the data from the free shareware demo.
- **[Jazz Jackrabbit](/en/port/jazz-jackrabbit-openjazz/)**: OpenJazz’s ([openjazz.github.io](https://openjazz.github.io/)) includes the shareware version of the first game and the Holiday Hare Christmas specials, which Epic MegaGames allowed people to copy under its shareware license.
- **[Cannon Fodder](/en/port/cannon-fodder-openfodder/)**: OpenFodder’s ([link](https://s3.amazonaws.com/openfodder/OpenFodder.html)) plays with the demos that magazines gave away at the time.
- **[Grand Theft Auto](/en/port/grand-theft-auto-carnage3d/)**: Carnage3D’s ([link](https://codenamecpp.github.io/carnage3d/web/carnage3D_wasm.html)) loads the data from an official demo of the game. Rockstar also once gave the original GTA away for free in its Rockstar Classics collection ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Theft_Auto_(video_game))).
- **[Super Mario Bros. X](/en/port/super-mario-bros-x-thextech/)**: TheXTech also publishes browser builds of this free fan game.

LEGO Island is the opposite case. Its [decompilation](/en/decomp/lego-island/) is in the catalog, but isle-portable, the port built on it, is not: its web version, [isle.pizza](https://isle.pizza), is run by the same team and plays without owning the game, because the site itself serves the game files (about 550 MB, which you can install for offline play). It’s the same line [we drew with the webports earlier this month](/en/blog/2026-10-12/): the catalog only lists what asks for your own copy.

## A catalog almost three times bigger

This week the catalog grew from 396 to 1,031 entries: recompilations from 161 to 452, ports from 60 to 351, decompilations from 132 to 156, tools from 37 to 43, and mods from 6 to 29. Many of them are projects that had been out for a while and weren’t listed yet.

The catalog also isn’t only about consoles anymore. It now covers reimplementations of PC games, like [OpenRA](/en/port/openra/), [OpenMW](/en/port/elder-scrolls-iii-morrowind-openmw/), or [OpenTTD](/en/port/transport-tycoon-deluxe-openttd/); ports of code released by the studios themselves, like Doom’s ([Chocolate Doom](/en/port/doom-chocolate-doom/), [GZDoom](/en/port/gzdoom/)) and Quake’s; free games, like [Warzone 2100](/en/port/warzone-2100/); and multi-game engines, like [ScummVM](/en/port/scummvm/). Decompilations are listed even when they don’t have a port yet.

**On the site:** there are new platforms: DOS, 32X / Super 32X, Mega-CD / Sega CD, PC-98, classic Mac OS, Mobile (Java ME, BREW, iOS, and Android), Neo Geo, Virtual Boy, Master System / Mark III, and Game Gear. The “PC” platform is now called “Windows.” There are two new port categories: “Game-specific engine,” for engines built for a single game that run its original code and move it to native code bit by bit, and “Source port,” for ports of code the studio itself released. Entries now show the previous owner when a repository changes hands, a tag when the project also releases builds for Android, iOS, Switch, 3DS, PS Vita, or the browser, and the progress of decompilations tracked by ByteMatch. Entries for projects taken down by DMCA stay, with no link to the code and with the notice: the first are [GTA III](/en/port/grand-theft-auto-iii-re3/) and [Vice City](/en/port/grand-theft-auto-vice-city-revc/) (re3 and reVC, taken down in 2021). And since October 20, the Terms spell it out: we don’t host anything, and we don’t link projects that ship protected assets unless their author states they have explicit permission from the rights holder.
