## God of War III Remastered, native on Windows

On October 10, at the very end of last week, Felipe Prado released the first version of a Windows port of God of War III Remastered for PS4 ([entry](/en/port/god-of-war-iii-remastered/), [GitHub](https://github.com/FelipePrado0/gow3_pc)). It comes from the same family as the Bloodborne port we covered last week: a fork of Supermedo’s Windows version, where the game’s original x86-64 code runs directly on the CPU and the graphics go to Vulkan through shadPS4’s video core.

This was the week it took off: on October 13 the Video Game Esoterica channel covered it ([video](https://youtu.be/M7bUqHo0Fkw)), and by October 14 it had 13 published versions, test builds included, and more than 120,000 downloads. According to its author, it’s playable from start to finish at native 1080p; at other resolutions some textures still look broken. You need your own PS4 dump of the game.

## Four more PS4 ports

God of War III wasn’t the only one. Over the following days, four more PS4 ports came out that work the same way: the game’s code runs directly on the PC’s CPU and the graphics are translated to Vulkan. All of them need your own dump of the game.

- **[Shadow of the Colossus](/en/port/shadow-of-the-colossus/)** (October 14): Bluepoint’s 2018 remake, with its own launcher, a 60 fps mode, a PS4 Pro mode, and output up to 4K. Its author ships fixes almost daily, and there are still known problems, for example with AMD graphics cards.
- **[Uncharted: The Nathan Drake Collection](/en/port/uncharted-the-nathan-drake-collection/)** (October 15): by Supermedo, the author of the Windows version of Bloodborne. All three games launch from one launcher, at 60 or 120 fps, with FSR or DLSS and frame generation. It passed 1,200 stars on GitHub in two days; problems remain, mostly in Uncharted 3.
- **[The Order: 1886](/en/port/the-order-1886/)** (October 15): still in development. It boots, lets you start a game, and plays the prologue at the game’s 30 fps, using shadPS4’s video core, like the God of War III port.
- **[Killzone Shadow Fall](/en/port/killzone-shadow-fall/)** (October 16): it’s in public beta, but the whole campaign can already be played from start to finish, at resolutions from 720p to 4K.

## Link’s Awakening, recompiled from the Switch

LAS Recomp, by peiikai, is a static recompilation of the Switch version of Link’s Awakening for Windows, Linux, and macOS ([entry](/en/recomp/links-awakening-switch/), [GitHub](https://github.com/peiikai/LASRecomp)). The recompilation happens on your PC the first time, from your own game files, and it adds an unlocked aspect ratio, 21:9 for example, and a randomizer whose seeds can be changed without restarting. It’s in beta: the third one came out on October 14, the same day Video Game Esoterica covered it ([video](https://youtu.be/8t598Kvb_Vo)), and the fourth on October 15. Its author warns that almost all of the code, tools, and documentation were written by an AI, Claude, through Claude Code.

It wasn’t the week’s only Switch project. On October 16, [ScarletNX](/en/tool/scarletnx/), an ARM64-to-x86 recompiler built from SwitchRecomp, published its first nightly build: you give it your dump, and it extracts the game, recompiles its code, and runs it. And [HWDER](/en/recomp/hyrule-warriors-definitive-edition/), for Hyrule Warriors: Definitive Edition, has been publishing test builds since October 3.

## Super Mario Galaxy: Nebula reaches 1.0

On October 13, keepvaibin released Nebula 1.0, a static recompilation of Super Mario Galaxy for Windows ([entry](/en/recomp/super-mario-galaxy/), [GitHub](https://github.com/keepvaibin/nebula)), a week after its first preview on October 6. The installer translates the game’s PowerPC code to C++ and compiles it on your PC from your own copy of the US version, so the download contains nothing from Nintendo.

The author still calls it a work in progress, and the README explains that the recompiler, the runtime, and the installer were written by AI agents under the author’s direction; the game’s logic, on the other hand, is the original code, translated instruction by instruction.

## Burnout 3: Takedown on Mac, Windows, Linux, and Steam Deck

Also on October 13, Takedawn 0.1.0 came out: a static recompilation of the Xbox version of Burnout 3: Takedown ([entry](/en/recomp/burnout-3-takedown/), [GitHub](https://github.com/xenophobentx/takedawn)). It builds on sp00nznet’s project and the xboxrecomp toolkit, and adds render scale up to 4x, a widescreen HUD fix, ultrawide, your own music, and 5.1 surround. The Android version will follow once a graphics bug found in the last tests is fixed. It joined the catalog through a pull request from its own author (#22).

## More news

- **[Shinobi III: Return of the Ninja Master](/en/port/shinobi-iii/)** (October 13): Ninja Master, a Rust reimplementation for Windows, Linux, and macOS that loads the graphics, music, and stages from your own cartridge. It’s experimental: all seven rounds are reachable, but a full playthrough isn’t guaranteed. With it, the Mega Drive / Genesis joins the catalog.
- **[Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time](/en/recomp/prince-of-persia-the-sands-of-time/)** (October 13): reSOT, a static recompilation of the Xbox builds, started the week with only the E3 2003 demo running. On October 16 its author pushed the NTSC retail version, which now reaches its first playable level, and a person has played the demo up to the third level. Both are still being tested, with crashes and sound pitched too high.
- **[Midnight Club 3: DUB Edition](/en/tool/xrevive/)** (October 13): with version 0.1.5 of xrevive, the launcher for Xbox games recompiled with Xrecomp, it becomes the first of them playable online, with an xb.live account. On October 14, 0.1.6 added a save editor and conversion of saves from the PS2 version. On October 15, Video Game Esoterica covered another game in the launcher, Ninja Gaiden Black, and 0.1.13, on October 16, gave it its own save editor.
- **[Crackdown](/en/recomp/crackdown-unowa001/)** (October 13): a second recomp of the Xbox 360 Crackdown, by Unowa001, also made with ReXGlue. It runs at 60 fps, comes with a launcher that installs the game from your own dump, and supports, experimentally, two-player co-op over System Link and an Archipelago randomizer. It’s in beta.
- **[Dead Rising 2: Case West](/en/recomp/dead-rising-2-case-west/)** (October 14): with version 1.1.2, its author calls this port of the Xbox 360-exclusive Dead Rising 2 epilogue finished. The campaign plays from start to finish, and two-player online co-op works through the XenonLive launcher. Active development has stopped, but the author encourages carrying it on through forks.
- **[Ratchet & Clank](/en/port/ratchet-and-clank/)**: ReRAC, a Rust rewrite of the PS2 game, has been releasing alpha builds since October 5, and Video Game Esoterica covered it on October 14. Every level loads, but it isn’t playable from start to finish yet.
- **[Resident Evil 3: Nemesis](/en/recomp/resident-evil-3/)** (October 12, at the very end of last week): Project RE-Visited, a static recompilation of the PS1 game for Windows. It extracts the executable from your own disc and adds analog controls, up to 240 fps, and 16:9; by October 17 it was already at version 1.4.4.
- **[Forza Horizon 2](/en/port/forza-horizon-2/)** (October 11, at the very end of last week): Azimuth runs the Xbox One version on Windows and Linux by translating the console’s calls to Win32 and DirectX 12. It’s the first Xbox One entry in the catalog; it’s published on itch.io, pay what you want and without source code.
- **[Forza Motorsport 5](/en/port/forza-motorsport-5/)** (October 15): Race One Hub, by LoZazaMastro, the second Xbox One entry, relies on an adapted version of Azimuth. It builds a Windows executable from your own copy, with resolutions up to 8K and up to 120 fps. Unlike Azimuth, it publishes the source code for its own part, under GPL-3.0.
- **Alley Cat and Saboteur II** (October 13 and 16): petarov released recompilations for Windows, Linux, and macOS of two DOS games, Alley Cat (1984) and Saboteur II: Avenging Angel (1987) ([Alley Cat](https://github.com/petarov/alleycat.exe), [Saboteur II](https://github.com/petarov/saboteur2)). They translate the original 8088 code to x86-64 assembly instead of emulating it, and add fullscreen and cheats; since October 17, Alley Cat can also be played [in the browser](https://petarov.github.io/alleycat.exe/). According to their READMEs, almost all of the code was generated by AI and only roughly reviewed. They’re not in the catalog because every download includes the original game.

Also new in the catalog: the recomps of [Mercenaries](/en/recomp/mercenaries/), [MediEvil](/en/recomp/medievil/), [Dead or Alive](/en/recomp/dead-or-alive/), [Banjo-Tooie](/en/recomp/banjo-tooie/), [TimeSplitters 2](/en/recomp/timesplitters-2/), and the first three Mega Drive Sonic games ([1](/en/recomp/sonic-the-hedgehog/), [2](/en/recomp/sonic-the-hedgehog-2/), and [3 & Knuckles](/en/recomp/sonic-3-and-knuckles/)), with their recompiler, [segagenesisrecomp](/en/tool/segagenesisrecomp/); the ports of [Crash Team Racing: Turbocharged](/en/port/crash-team-racing-turbocharged/), [Star Fox Adventures](/en/port/star-fox-adventures/), and [sm64coopdx](/en/port/sm64coopdx/); and the [OutRun2006Tweaks](/en/mod/outrun-2006-tweaks/) mod. The [Namco System 21 / 22 / 23](/en/decomp/namco-system-22/) decompilation now covers ten arcade games, including both Time Crisis titles.

Then came the first three PS3 game entries, all made with ps3recomp: [Drakengard 3](/en/recomp/drakengard-3/), in alpha and running at 60 fps at 4K on a high-end PC; [Disgaea D2](/en/recomp/disgaea-d2/); and [The Simpsons Arcade Game](/en/recomp/simpsons-arcade-game-ps3/), which has a sibling project for [Xbox 360](/en/recomp/simpsons-arcade-game/). Also new: [Tekken 6](/en/recomp/tekken-6/) and [Mortal Kombat vs. DC Universe](/en/recomp/mortal-kombat-vs-dc-universe/), on Xbox 360, the first one recompiled for online fights with rollback; [Mortal Kombat 4](/en/recomp/mortal-kombat-4/), [Mortal Kombat Trilogy](/en/recomp/mortal-kombat-trilogy/), and [Evil Zone](/en/recomp/evil-zone/), on PS1; the exact decompilations of [BattleTanx](/en/decomp/battletanx/) and [BattleTanx: Global Assault](/en/decomp/battletanx-global-assault/), on Nintendo 64, with the second one’s [PC port](/en/recomp/battletanx-global-assault-pc-port/), through a pull request from their own author (#26); and the [Crash Team Racing decompilation](/en/decomp/crash-team-racing/), along with [High Octane](/en/port/crash-team-racing-high-octane/), the other port that comes from it.

**On the site:** since October 17, Recompendium has its own domain: [recompendium.com](https://recompendium.com/). The old addresses, on nio03.github.io/unricopie, lead to the same page on the new domain automatically. Every console also has its own page now, with everything there is for it, like [Nintendo 64](/en/consola/n64/) or [Xbox 360](/en/consola/x360/); entries carry an “AI-assisted” or “human-driven” tag when the project itself says so (49 and 23 entries, after reading the README of every project in the catalog); consoles with two regional names show both, like NES / Famicom or Mega Drive / Genesis; the menu collapses on phones and tablets, and addresses that don’t exist lead to a proper page in both languages.

**License change:** since October 17, the catalog entries and the blog texts are released under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), the same license Wikipedia uses; the site’s code stays under MIT. The reason is the work behind every entry: 393 projects described by hand in two languages, each checked against its repository and corrected when what’s announced doesn’t match what the README says. Until now all of that was under MIT, a license designed for software, which allows anyone to copy the whole catalog and close it off without giving anything back. Other catalogs have already grown out of this one, and that’s fine: that’s what an open catalog is for. With CC BY-SA it stays open, commercial use included, on two conditions. First, credit Recompendium with a link, so anyone who arrives through a copy can find the up-to-date version. Second, share what you build from it under the same license, so improvements flow back to the community instead of staying on another site. Everything published before the change remains available under MIT ([details](https://github.com/Nio03/unricopie/blob/main/LICENSE-DATA)).
