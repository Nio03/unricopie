Recompendium is a catalog: each entry tells you what a project is, what state it’s in, and what you need to play it. What an entry can’t tell you is what’s happening in the scene: what came out, what went down, which project changed hands, or which story doesn’t fit anywhere else. That’s what this blog is for.

## What you’ll find here

- **A weekly log**, with dates: the projects that join the catalog, the changes that matter, and what gets taken down.
- **News from the scene**, even when the project can’t join the catalog. This week’s story is the best example.
- **Opinion, now and then**, always labeled as such.
- **Sources linked throughout.** If something isn’t confirmed, we say so.

## The web-port craze

Between September 30 and October 10, 2026, the scene filled up with browser ports: console and PC games that opened in a tab, with nothing to install. Nearly all of them were built with the help of AI agents, and nearly all of them were short-lived.

**September 30: Halo: Combat Evolved.** A browser port with multiplayer that, according to XDA, logged around 20,000 plays in its first five hours. XDA placed it in the same family of projects as the [Halo decompilation](/unricopie/en/decomp/halo-combat-evolved/) and [Halo CE Universal](/unricopie/en/port/halo-combat-evolved/), both in the catalog ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/you-can-now-play-the-original-halo-for-free-in-your-browser-with-128-player-multiplayer/)). Its address is now blocked.

**October 6: GTA V.** [playgta5.com](https://playgta5.com) let you play Grand Theft Auto V in the browser for free. It went viral, and within hours it no longer worked ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/rockstar-shuts-down-free-gta-5-browser-port/), [Tom’s Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/video-games/pc-gaming/gta-v-playable-in-browser-immediately-nuked-unofficial-webassembly-port-built-with-ai-gets-taken-down-within-hours-of-going-live)). Press reports credited Take-Two with the shutdown and said the port was built from Rockstar’s leaked source code, with the game’s assets compressed to about 700 MB and AI used to bring it to WebAssembly. None of that is confirmed: Take-Two hasn’t said anything, no DMCA notice has been published, and no one has claimed the project. The page now returns an error.

**October 8: Super Mario 64.** Oliver Sauter released a browser port at [olivers.tools/play](https://olivers.tools/play) with 12-player multiplayer and voice and video chat. He built it with AI assistance on top of sm64ex, a fork of [sm64-port](/unricopie/en/port/sm64-port/) ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/you-can-now-play-super-mario-64-in-browser-with-12-player-multiplayer/)). At first it simply ran; since October 10 it asks you to load your own ROM.

**October 8–9: the list grows.** A thread on X rounded up more ports you could play “without the game installed”: Call of Duty: Black Ops, Halo, GTA: Vice City, Skate 3, and The Simpsons: Hit & Run. Kotaku tried several the next day and found some of them nearly flawless; it credited AI models that now decompile far faster than a person can ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/we-might-be-cooked-as-these-vibe-coded-web-browser-ports-of-halo-the-simpsons-hit-and-run-and-gta-vice-city-seem-to-work-perfectly-2000743300)).

**October 10: Call of Duty.** Luckey Faraday announced that Activision had sent him DMCA notices and that all of his games were offline ([his post on X](https://x.com/luckeyfaraday/status/2108722723849379986)). There were two: *Claude of Duty: Vibe Slops II*, the Hijacked map from Black Ops II multiplayer with AI-controlled enemies, and *Kino der Toten*, the Black Ops zombies map. He built them with Three.js and AI agents, and according to their own pages they used maps, models, and animations exported from the original PC games ([XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/activision-hits-vibe-coded-classic-call-of-duty-browser-ports-with-dmca-takedowns/), [TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/353601/activision-dmca-takes-down-vibe-coded-call-of-duty-browser-ports)). Their page, [vibeslops.luckeysystems.com](https://vibeslops.luckeysystems.com/), now shows only that announcement. Activision has not commented.

### Why they aren’t in the catalog

It isn’t because they run on the web. The catalog only lists projects that ask for your own copy of the game, and GTA V and the Call of Duty ports shipped the game inside.

Super Mario 64 shows exactly where the line is, because it’s the same port before and after October 10. While it served the game, it was out; now that it asks for your ROM, it’s the kind of browser port that can have a place here.

Whatever can’t join the catalog gets covered on this blog, with its pages and its sources.

## New this week

22 new projects: 9 recompilations, 7 ports, 3 decompilations, and 3 tools. The catalog now stands at 343 entries across 24 platforms.

### Highlights

**[Bloodborne](/unricopie/en/port/bloodborne/)** arrived with over 2,600 stars and forced us to create a new category. It’s neither a recompilation nor a decompilation: its author calls it “the counterpart of Wine + DXVK for a single game.” The original PS4 executable runs directly on the CPU, and a runtime written for this one game replaces the system libraries. We file it as a **native layer**. A fork brings it to Windows with DLSS and beta online play.

**[AnyPS5](/unricopie/en/tool/anyps5/)** became the most-starred project in the entire catalog. It applies the same idea as Bloodborne to any PS5 executable, though it still covers only part of the system libraries.

**[P.T.](/unricopie/en/port/pt/)** is an unusual case: the teaser’s logic was rewritten in C++ based on how the original behaved, and everything else is read from your own PS4 copy.

### Firsts

- **[Tokyo Bus Guide](/unricopie/en/decomp/tokyo-bus-guide/)**: the first public decompilation of a Dreamcast game.
- **[Arcade Daytona USA](/unricopie/en/recomp/daytona-usa-arcade/)**: recompiles both the i960 program and the TGP coprocessor’s program on the Model 2 board. It’s a separate project from the recompilation of the Xbox 360 version we already had.
- **[ICO](/unricopie/en/decomp/ico/)**: a decompilation that matches the PAL disc byte for byte, plus [its port](/unricopie/en/port/ico/) to PC and Android.

### Everything else

**Recompilations:** [Wind Waker HD](/unricopie/en/recomp/wind-waker-hd/) (the Wii U version; the GameCube one was already in the catalog), [Sonic Adventure](/unricopie/en/recomp/sonic-adventure/), [Spider-Man: Edge of Time](/unricopie/en/recomp/spider-man-edge-of-time/), [Forza Horizon](/unricopie/en/recomp/forza-horizon/), [Ridge Racer 6](/unricopie/en/recomp/ridge-racer-6/), [Dead or Alive Xtreme Beach Volleyball](/unricopie/en/recomp/dead-or-alive-xtreme-beach-volleyball/), [Rush 2](/unricopie/en/recomp/rush-2/), and [EarthBound / Mother 2](/unricopie/en/recomp/earthbound/).

**Ports built on decompilations:** [Metroid Prime](/unricopie/en/port/metroid-prime/), [Super Mario Sunshine](/unricopie/en/port/super-mario-sunshine/), [Sly Cooper](/unricopie/en/port/sly-cooper-projectcane/), and [Dragon Ball Z: Budokai Tenkaichi 3](/unricopie/en/port/dragon-ball-z-budokai-tenkaichi-3/).

**Decompilations:** [Resident Evil for PC (1997)](/unricopie/en/decomp/resident-evil-1-pc/), the only entry in the catalog whose original game is a PC game.

**Tools:** [Xrecomp](/unricopie/en/tool/xrecomp/), a recompiler for original Xbox games, and [xrevive](/unricopie/en/tool/xrevive/), its launcher with online play.

## Changes to the site

- The catalog now brings all 343 entries together in a single list, filterable by type. Recompilations have their own page.
- After a heads-up on Reddit, we rewrote the descriptions of 116 of the 119 decompilations whose Spanish text was identical to the English, never translated. Three remain.
- A new **“stalled”** label for projects that stopped before they were finished. Finished projects that are simply quiet don’t get it.
- An RSS feed in Spanish and English.
- Something went wrong: **from September 30 to October 8, the site didn’t update.** A stuck deployment was blocking every one after it, so new entries existed but weren’t visible. It’s fixed.
